GençKart is a family banking concept for PTT. It brings 180 years of PTT trust together with the digital habits of Gen Z, in one app with two experiences: full control and security for parents, and a playful way for teens to learn to manage money.

01Problem

Parents need to see, limit and approve. Teens need room to spend, save and learn. Both share one account, so the design has to serve them without one undermining the other.

02What I did

I led the research, from competitor analysis to the information architecture, and designed the interface.

Competitive analysis · Information architecture · UI design

01.1 Challenge

One app, two different experiences

[ Design principles ]
01

Gamified interaction

Money actions feed into a loop of XP, levels and badges, so banking becomes a habit instead of a chore.

02

Active earning loop

Allowance stops being a passive gift. “Complete the task → earn it → get paid” teaches the value of work early.

03

Smart request protocol

Category-based, transparent and fast approvals take the tension out of asking parents for money.

04

Financial literacy

Videos, an interactive glossary and quizzes with rewards add theory to everyday spending.

01.2UX research

Five teen banking products, side by side

I ran a competitive analysis of five global teen and family banking apps. For each one I documented its features, its parental-control model and what sets it apart.

[ Method ]Competitive analysis
[ Products ]Greenlight · GoHenry · Revolut <18 · Step · Current
[ Outputs ]Feature matrix · Opportunity map · Information architecture
[ Key finding // Parental-control models ]

Who holds the controls?

The products differ most in how much the parent decides.

← Full parent controlTeen independence →
GreenlightThe parent opens and manages the teen account and owns every permission. The teen is the second user.
CurrentSupervision plus limits. Parents set limits, get notifications and can freeze the card, but don’t approve every transaction.
StepA shared co-pilot account. The parent acts as a coach, sets limits only, and there is no per-transaction approval.

01.3 — From research to design

Patterns that became screens

Some features were standard across the competitors. Here is how each one appears in GençKart.

Seen in research→ In GençKart
01Chores paid on completionGoHenry · Greenlight · Revolut <18

Tasks have a reward, a due date and a status such as “Onay bekliyor” (awaiting approval). Parents mark a task complete or delete it.

02Category spending limitsGoHenry · Greenlight

A weekly total limit, plus separate limits for games, food and shopping.

03Automatic allowanceGreenlight · Revolut <18

Set the amount, how often and the start day, then confirm on a summary screen.

04Gamified financial educationGoHenry · Greenlight

Academy courses such as “Para Temelleri” (money basics), each split into short lessons that unlock in order.

05Goal-based savingsRevolut <18 · Step · GoHenry

Goals with a progress bar and a “Katkı yap” (contribute) action, plus target piggy banks in Savings.

06Instant card freezeRevolut <18 · Current

One toggle freezes the card and tells the parent that all transactions will be declined.

07Spending insightsRevolut <18 · Current

A spending analysis by category, and a brand analysis ranking where money goes.

[ Opportunities ]

12 ways GençKart can stand apart

I compared the competitor findings with what PTT already has and listed 12 differentiators. Most rely on assets no global teen card has: public backing, a physical network and local partnerships.

Trust & public reach03
01 State guarantee + branch network81 provinces and 5,000+ branches give every teen and parent physical support.↳ No global teen card has public backing.
02 Ministry integrationScholarships, aid and social support from the Ministry of National Education (MEB) and the Ministry of Family and Social Services go to the card automatically.↳ No teen card worldwide has this integration model.
12 Public financial-literacy programmeFinance modules for young people in Turkey, run with schools and municipalities.↳ Greenlight has modules, but nothing publicly backed.
Play & rewards04
03 Savings GalaxyPlanets, light years, supernova multipliers and a galaxy map.↳ Greenlight and GoHenry have goals, but no themed universe.
04 Digital piggy bankRound-up savings, mini rewards and a fill-up animation.↳ GoHenry has simple savings; the visuals and micro-rewards set this apart.
05 Two-way rewardsStars from parents, badges for teens, and conversion into gift balance.↳ This hybrid model doesn’t exist globally.
09 Personalisation universe100+ card designs, app themes, seasonal designs and community voting.
Local ecosystem04
06 Turkey-specific integrationsPaying at school canteens, QR payments at neighbourhood shops, and PTTAVM products as savings goals.↳ No competitor has a local ecosystem this broad.
07 Bring-your-subscriptions campaignA bonus that grows as Spotify, Netflix and other subscriptions move to the card.↳ Step and Current do something similar; no youth product in Turkey does.
10 National brand partnershipsGoal-linked discounts with Nike, Teknosa, D&R and gaming platforms.
11 PTT Cargo integrationSave, buy the product, and receive it through PTT delivery.↳ No one else covers the whole chain from goal to delivery.
Control01
08 Parent control layer + emergency fund lock“Rainy-day money” that only unlocks with the parent’s password.↳ Even Greenlight has no clearly defined emergency fund lock.

01 · Onboarding & entry

Three intro screens set out the promise. The landing screen then splits teens (“Yolculuğu Başlat”, start the journey) from parents (“Ebeveyn Girişi”, parent login).

02 · Home & quick send

The family balance first

Home opens on the total family balance. Each child has a card showing how much of their limit is left, and a + button sends money straight from there. A swipe confirms each transfer.

03 · Family management

One control centre per child

Each child’s screen shows their level, wallet and savings, then three tabs: Görevler (tasks), Akademi (academy) and Hedefler (goals). Card controls are grouped in settings at the bottom.

04 · Allowance & limits

Setting an allowance takes three steps: amount and frequency, start day, then a summary to confirm. Limits work at two levels: one weekly total, plus a limit for each category.

05 · Analysis

Spending, savings and progress

The Analysis tab brings together category spending, brand analysis, active campaigns, savings growth, a spending timeline, task performance and education progress. It can be viewed weekly, monthly or yearly.

06 · Profile, security & support

Profile has quick links to cards, limits, statements and support, followed by family banking, security and general settings.

07 · Dark theme · English

A dark version of the key parent screens, in English, with lime as the accent colour.
